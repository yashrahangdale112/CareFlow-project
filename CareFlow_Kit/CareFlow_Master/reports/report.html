1. Executive summary

CareFlow analyses a hospital event log with process-mining techniques and answers three operational questions for the hospital: what is the real care pathway, where does time and money leak, and which patient will become a long stay. From 1,500 patient journeys and 13,583 clean events the project reconstructs 14 pathway variants on a 28-edge Directly-Follows Graph, locates the worst bottleneck (Observation, 226.6 minutes average wait), shows that Operation Theatre consumes the largest share of cost (INR 9,381,243 of INR 25,886,761), and trains a long-stay classifier whose ranking quality (AUC ≈ 0.998 on the leakage-free feature set) beats a naive baseline. Everything is reproducible with one command and is published as code, SQL, charts, a dashboard, a report and a presentation.

2. Problem statement & objectives

Clinical pathways are designed on paper but executed differently every day: lab tests repeat, patients queue, some journeys escalate to ICU. Hospitals usually see only aggregate totals, not the sequence. CareFlow applies process mining to close that gap.

  • O1 Discover the real pathway as a process model (DFG + variants).
  • O2 Quantify bottlenecks, waiting time, service time, cost and resource load.
  • O3 Measure conformance of actual cases against the expected pathway.
  • O4 Predict long stay (LOS > 24 h) from leakage-free features.
  • O5 Deliver an interactive dashboard and full documentation to industry standards.

6.4 Model evaluation (long stay > 24 h)

Two feature sets were compared on purpose. The full set contains post-outcome columns (gap/wait aggregates that only exist after the stay ends) and therefore scores a perfect 1.000 — a textbook leakage artefact. The leakage-free set (age, gender, triage level, admission type, pathway, event count) is the one shipped as the production model.

Honest reading: the long-stay class is only 1.13 % of cases (17 of 1,500), so accuracy is misleading and cross-validated F1 swings widely — the model ranks risk well (AUC 0.998) but is not yet a clinically reliable decision rule. Recommended next step: collect more long-stay cases or use cost-sensitive learning / SMOTE, and validate on a real log where AUC ≈ 0.75–0.90 is expected.

7. Business recommendations

  • Fix Observation first. At 226.6 minutes average wait it is the single largest delay in the pathway; adding one observation bay or a queue rule recovers more time per rupee than any other change.
  • Schedule into Friday afternoon wisely. Friday 14:00 is peak load; shift elective activity away from that slot to reduce waits.
  • Audit Operation Theatre utilisation. 386 events produce 36 % of total cost — the best target for cost review (bundling, consumables, theatre turnaround).
  • Kill rework at the source. Repeated activities (rework events) inflate both LOS and cost; standardise the top repeated step.
  • Route Emergency Assessment cases onto a fast track. Emergency Assessment → Lab Test (579 cases) is the highest-volume escalation edge and the natural place for a pre-approved lab panel.
  • Flag risk early. Use the gradient-boosting risk score at triage to prioritise case management for probable long stays.

8. Limitations & next steps

  • Reference log is synthetic; real-log fitness is expected in 0.7–0.95, not 1.0.
  • Cost is activity-level, not invoice-level; no payer or insurance dimension.
  • Extreme class imbalance (1.13 %) limits classifier reliability.
  • Next: connect a real hospital log (Sepsis Cases or HIS export), add pm4py Inductive Miner with Graphviz for the Petri net, deploy the dashboard, and add a drift monitor for the process model.
